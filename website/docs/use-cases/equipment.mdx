## A free-weight bar, and a pin-loaded stack

A barbell is identity, classification, and the localized and vendor-specific detail
a gym wants — but no loading rule, because a bar takes any plates.

<SpecExample source="specification/schemas/equipment/v1.1.0/equipment.example.json" data={barbell}>
  A barbell. The `x:gym-management` extension carries inventory and maintenance a
  consumer can ignore; the core stays about the equipment itself.
</SpecExample>

A pin-loaded cable stack is different: its load changes in fixed steps, and that is
a fact a prescription downstream needs to round to.

<SpecExample source="specification/schemas/equipment/v1.1.0/equipment.example.stack.json" data={cableStack}>
  `loading.increment` is 5 kg and `stackBased` is true — the increments are fixed,
  so a consumer knows a prescription must snap to them.
</SpecExample>

## How it works

`loading` is where reproducibility lives: the increment and whether it is stack-based
are enough to do plate or pin math the same way in every consumer. Resistance that
changes *how hard* rather than *what* — a machine dial, a resistance level — stays a
load prescription with a named scale, not an equipment fact, because two
manufacturers' "level 7" are unrelated and the standard refuses to imply otherwise.

The rule of thumb the model enforces: record what is portable as numbers, and keep
what is not portable clearly marked as machine-specific, so a session is reproducible
on the same machine and never silently mis-converted onto a different one.
