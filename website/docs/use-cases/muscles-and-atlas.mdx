# Bind muscles to a body without owning the artwork

> Name a region once; every renderer draws it, and none has to agree on the drawing.

<Audience ids={['app-developer', 'researcher']} />

<BeforeAfter>
  <Panel kind="before">
    Each app hard-codes a mapping from a muscle to a path in one particular SVG.
    Commission a new illustration and the anatomy has to be redone; share a
    highlight between two apps and it does not line up, because neither agrees on
    the drawing underneath.
  </Panel>
  <Panel kind="after">
    A muscle names the regions it occupies as ids and a weight. An atlas maps each
    region to a selector inside a view. Swap the artwork, keep the semantics — the
    binding is an indirection layer, and the geometry stays out of the data.
  </Panel>
</BeforeAfter>

## Three records that compose

A muscle carries a `heatmap` naming atlas regions by id, each with a weight —
never a path, never a colour.

<SpecExample source="specification/schemas/muscle/v1.0.0/muscle.example.json" data={quadriceps}>
  The quadriceps, bound to two atlas areas at full weight. `classification.categoryId`
  points at the category below.
</SpecExample>

A muscle category groups muscles for the reader, and is itself just an id, a name,
and classification.

<SpecExample source="specification/schemas/muscle/muscle-category/v1.0.0/muscle-category.example.json" data={legs}>
  The "Legs" category the quadriceps belongs to, referenced by `cat.legs`.
</SpecExample>

The atlas resolves those region ids to selectors inside named views — and it ships
no geometry itself. The drawing lives behind the `asset.uri`; the atlas is the map
from a region id to a selector in it.

<SpecExample source="specification/schemas/atlas/v1.0.0/body-atlas.example.json" data={atlas}>
  Four areas, each binding a `viewId` and a `selector` such as `#area-thigh-left`.
  The selector points into *your* SVG — the standard commits to none.
</SpecExample>

## How it works

The indirection is the feature. A muscle says *which regions and how strongly*; an
atlas says *where those regions are in a given view*; the artwork is yours and
interchangeable. Because no coordinate or path lives in the entity records, a muscle
library is cacheable and diffable, and a new illustration is a new atlas binding
rather than an edit to the anatomy.

Note what the standard deliberately does **not** ship: this example atlas carries four
areas and no drawing — `asset.uri` points at an asset the atlas does not define, and
aggregation across weights is advisory, not mandated. The map is normative; the picture
is an implementation choice.
