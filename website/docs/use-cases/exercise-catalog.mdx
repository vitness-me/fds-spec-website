## A complete exercise

Everything a consumer needs to place a squat in a library: canonical name and
slug, localized names, movement and mechanics classification, the muscles it
targets with their category ids, the equipment it needs, and the metrics it is
measured in. Vendor-specific extras live under the `x:` namespace, out of the
way of the core.

<SpecExample source="specification/schemas/exercises/v1.1.0/exercise.example.json" data={backSquat}>
  A back squat, fully described. Note `targets.primary[].id` — a muscle id another
  document resolves — and the `x:vitness.*` extension keys, which a consumer that
  does not know them ignores.
</SpecExample>

## How it works

The identity is a stable id plus a canonical name and slug; every human-facing
string can be localized without touching the id. `classification` carries the
movement facts a consumer filters and groups on. `targets` names muscles by id
rather than by free text, so an exercise catalog and a muscle library resolve
against each other — the chain from an exercise to a body region is real, not
described.

Fields FDS did not anticipate do not force a fork: they go under the `x:vendor`
extension namespace, and a consumer that has never heard of them still validates
the document. That is what lets one catalog serve many readers without any of
them agreeing in advance on everything it might carry.
